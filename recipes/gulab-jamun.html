Gulab Jamun Recipe

Gulab Jamun is a classic Indian dessert featuring soft, round dumplings made from a milk-based dough that are deep-fried to a rich, golden-brown color. Once fried, these warm spheres are soaked in a sticky, sweet sugar syrup infused with aromatic cardamom and rose water. The result is a melt-in-your-mouth delicacy that is incredibly juicy, intensely fragrant, and a staple at festivals and celebrations.

Ingredients:

For the Sugar Syrup

  • sugar: 1.5 cups
  • water: 1.5 cups
  • green cardamoms (lightly crushed): 4-5
  • rose water: 1 tsp

For the Gulab Jamun Dough

  • milk powder 1 cup
  • all-purpose flour (maida):1/4 cup
  • baking soda: 1/4 tsp
  • ghee (melted) 2 tbsp
  • milk (or as needed to knead): 3 to 4 tbsp

For Frying

  • Ghee or neutral oil for deep frying

Instructions:

  1. Make the Sugar Syrup
    1. Combine sugar and water in a pan over medium heat.
    2. Add crushed cardamom and saffron.
    3. Boil for about 5 minutes until it feels slightly sticky (do not make a single-string syrup).
    4. Turn off the heat and stir in the rose water. Keep the syrup warm.
  2. Prepare the Dough
    1. In a mixing bowl, combine the milk powder, all-purpose flour, and baking soda.
    2. Add the melted ghee and mix with your fingers until crumbly.
    3. Slowly add milk a little at a time to form a soft, smooth, and crack-free dough. Do not over-knead.
    4. Cover and let it rest for 10 minutes.
  3. Shape the Balls
    1. Grease your hands with a little ghee. Divide the dough into small, equal portions (about 14–18 balls).
    2. Roll them gently between your palms to ensure there are no cracks, as cracks will cause them to break while frying
  4. Deep Fry
    1. Heat ghee or oil in a deep pan over low to medium-low heat. The oil should not be too hot, or the outside will burn while the inside stays raw.
    2. Drop a small piece of dough in to test: it should rise slowly and steadily.
    3. Gently add the balls into the oil and swirl the oil around so they brown evenly.
    4. Fry on low heat until they turn a deep golden-brown color. Remove with a slotted spoon.
  5. 5. Soak in Syrup
    1. Drop the hot fried jamuns directly into the warm sugar syrup.
    2. Let them soak for at least 1 to 3 hours so they absorb the liquid and turn soft and spongy.
